About us

Jewish life in Kassel dates back to the Middle Ages: by the late 14th century there was an organised community with a synagogue and cemetery. In the 18th and 19th centuries Kassel became the seat of the regional rabbinate, and in 1839 the great synagogue on Untere Königsstraße was consecrated.

In November 1938 the synagogues of Kassel were destroyed. In 1941 and 1942 hundreds of Kassel Jews were deported to Riga, Majdanek and Theresienstadt. We keep their memory alive.

After the war the community was re-established. Since the 1990s it has grown considerably through immigration from the former Soviet Union. In 2000 the new synagogue on Bremer Straße, designed by architect Alfred Jacoby, was consecrated.

Today the community has about 650–850 members (to verify). It is a member of the Association of Jewish Communities in Hesse.
